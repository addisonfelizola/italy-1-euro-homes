Can foreigners buy a €1 house in Italy?

Usually yes, with conditions. Here is what to check whether you are from the EU, the United States, the United Kingdom, or elsewhere.

The short answer

Most sources say foreign buyers, including non-EU citizens, can take part in the schemes. Requirements vary by town, so the town’s notice has the final word.

You will need an Italian tax code

Any foreign buyer needs an Italian tax code (codice fiscale), which is issued through the Italian Revenue Agency. It is required for any real estate transaction.

Ownership does not give residency

Owning a €1 house does not give you residency rights in Italy. If you are not an EU citizen, visa rules still apply, so plan your residence separately.

The reciprocity question

Some legal guides say that when a non-resident foreigner buys property in Italy, the purchase can depend on an international treaty of reciprocity between Italy and the buyer’s home country. Other guides say there are no restrictions for non-residents. The sources disagree, so ask an Italian lawyer to confirm the position for your nationality before you apply.

Language and paperwork

Notices and contracts are in Italian. A plain-English summary is helpful for understanding, but it is not a legal translation. Legal documents usually need a sworn or certified translation, so ask your lawyer which translations you need.

Renting the house out

Using the house as a tourist rental is generally allowed, but you should tell the local authority about your plans when you apply. Check the notice and local rules before you assume it is permitted.

Before you apply

  • Get an Italian tax code.
  • Read the full notice, with its annexes, in Italian or with a translator.
  • Talk to an Italian lawyer or commercialista about your nationality and plans.
  • Get contractor quotes before you commit to a renovation budget.
