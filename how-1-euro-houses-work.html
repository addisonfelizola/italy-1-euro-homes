How €1 houses in Italy work

Italy’s “ houses” are €1 homes: old properties that towns sell for one euro, roughly a dollar, to bring people back to their historic centers. The price is symbolic. The obligations are real.

Where the houses come from

Owners of unused, often derelict buildings give them to the municipality. The town collects the properties, publishes a public notice (bando) listing them with the conditions, and promotes them to buyers.

Why towns do it

Many small towns in Italy have lost residents, leaving empty homes in their historic centers. Selling houses for €1 is meant to attract families, young people, businesses, and tourist operators who will restore the buildings and live or work in them.

Where it happens

Sicily and Sardinia have the most schemes, but towns in other regions have joined too. Schemes open and close as towns publish new calls or assign properties, and some pause for years. The most reliable check is the town’s own website, since no single national list is kept up to date.

The general process

Details vary by town, but most notices follow a similar sequence:

  1. Find an open notice and read all of it, including the annexes. Notices are in Italian.
  2. Check eligibility. Some towns limit who can apply, and conditions differ widely.
  3. Submit your application and the documents the notice requires, which may include a proposed renovation project and proof of financial capacity.
  4. Pay any deposit or provide the required guarantee.
  5. Sign the purchase deed, then submit the renovation plan within the town’s deadline.
  6. Start the work once permits are issued, and finish it on time.
  7. The town inspects the finished house, and the deposit is returned if the conditions were met.

Fixed prices and auctions

Not every notice works the same way. Some sell at a fixed price of €1. Others use a public auction where bids start at €1 and can rise. In an auction, the house can cost more than €1, so read the notice to see which method applies.

Important limits

  • There is no national rulebook. Each town sets its own conditions.
  • Deadlines are strict. Missing them can cost you the deposit or bring penalties.
  • Resale can be restricted until the renovation obligations are met, so this is not a quick flip.
  • Buying does not guarantee residency or visa rights, even when the purchase is complete.
