What a €1 house really costs

The €1 is the purchase price, not the total cost. Renovation is usually the largest expense, and it depends heavily on the house.

Renovation costs

Estimates vary widely. One source puts total costs at roughly €30,000 to €100,000 or more, and another gives €50,000 to €150,000 for a straightforward renovation. Get at least two contractor quotes before you commit, and ask specifically about structural work, which matters most in earthquake-prone areas.

Deposits and guarantees

Many towns require a security deposit or an insurance guarantee, commonly somewhere between about €1,000 and €5,000 depending on the town. It is normally returned once the renovation is finished and inspected. If the work isn’t completed on time, the notice may let the town keep it.

Renovation deadlines

Timelines are set by each town. A 2022 notice in Calatafimi-Segesta, Sicily, required a renovation plan within three months of signing the deed, work to start within three months of the building permit, and completion within three years, with extensions only if the town approved them. Penne, in Abruzzo, gave buyers three years to finish. Other towns set shorter or longer windows.

Costs beyond the renovation

  • Notary, registration, and legal fees on the purchase. One 2024 guide puts legal fees alone at up to about €3,000.
  • Utility connections. Water, gas, electricity, and sewer connections can be a separate cost.
  • Permits, surveys, and engineering reports, including energy performance certificates and compliance checks.
  • Currency fees. Card payments to Italian contractors from a non-euro account can add up, so compare transfer options.
  • Furnishing and running costs once the house is livable.

Penalties

Some notices set penalties for missed deadlines, including losing the deposit. Read the penalty clause before you apply or bid.

A practical habit: budget for the renovation plus a contingency buffer. Old buildings often reveal problems once work starts, and that is where most budgets break.
