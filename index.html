€1

The price is only the starting point.

Before you buy, know this.

Each town sets its own rules.

There is no national framework. Deadlines, deposits, and conditions come from each town’s notice.

The work is the real commitment.

Most of these houses need major renovation, and towns often require the work to finish within about three years.

A deposit is often required.

Many towns ask for a guarantee that is refunded when the renovation is complete, or kept if it isn’t.

Questions people ask.

What are $1 houses in Italy?

They are Italy’s €1 houses: old, often derelict properties that towns sell for one euro, roughly a dollar, to revive depopulated historic centers. Buyers must renovate them, so the €1 is not the total cost. Read the guide.

Can foreigners buy a €1 house in Italy?

Most sources say yes, including non-EU citizens. Buyers need an Italian tax code, and owning a house does not grant residency. Read the full answer.

How much does a €1 house really cost?

Renovation estimates range widely, from roughly €30,000 to €150,000 depending on the property. Towns also usually require a deposit, commonly about €1,000 to €5,000, normally returned when the work is complete. See the breakdown.

Are €1 house schemes still running in 2026?

Yes. Several towns in Sicily, Sardinia, and other regions still run schemes, but they open and close, so check each town’s current notice before applying.
